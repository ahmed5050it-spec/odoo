# maintenance — Reverse-Engineering Brief

The **Maintenance** module (`maintenance`, category *Supply Chain/Maintenance*,
`application=True`) tracks physical **equipment/assets** and manages the
**maintenance requests** raised against them. It is **not** auto-installed and
sits at the very base of its graph: it depends only on `mail`. Its center of
gravity is the `maintenance.equipment` ↔ `maintenance.request` pair, organized
into a kanban pipeline of user-defined `maintenance.stage`s and reliability
KPIs (MTBF/MTTR) carried by the `maintenance.mixin` abstract model.

## Role & Dependencies

| Depends on | Why (inferred) |
|------------|----------------|
| `mail` | Chatter, followers, and **activities** on equipment/requests; the team `mail.alias.mixin` lets inbound email create requests. |

**Adds on top:** an equipment register (category/serial/warranty/cost/scrap), a
corrective-vs-preventive request lifecycle on a kanban pipeline, recurring
preventive scheduling, maintenance teams with a dashboard, and MTBF/MTTR
reliability analytics. No accounting/stock coupling out of the box.

**Central model:** `maintenance.request` (`_order='id desc'`) — everything
points at it. `maintenance.mixin` is a **mixin** (`AbstractModel`, `_inherit`
without an own table) that injects the reliability KPIs into
`maintenance.equipment`. The field-type mix is **relational + scheduling**:
`Many2one` (17) and `Integer` (19, mostly KPI/dashboard counters) dominate,
with `Date`/`Datetime` (11) for request/close/schedule timing — an
asset-tracking, time-series-flavored model rather than a money document.

## Value-Configuration Classification

**Value model: `support` · activity_class: `support`.** Per Stabell &
Fjeldstad, maintenance is a **firm-infrastructure support activity**. Its
internal logic is value-*shop*-shaped (diagnose a failure → assign a technician
→ repair → close), but it is classified **support** because it sustains the
firm's productive *assets* rather than transforming a customer order — it buys,
makes, and sells nothing tradeable. It keeps equipment serviceable so the
primary chain (mrp/stock) can run; depending only on `mail` and exposing no
order/invoice document confirms the cross-cutting, infrastructure posture.

## APQC PCF Hint

**10.0 — Acquire, Construct, and Manage Assets** (asset maintenance). The
corrective request lifecycle maps to *10.5 Maintain productive assets*, and the
recurring preventive scheduling maps to the *plan/schedule preventive
maintenance* sub-processes. Category **8.x "Manage IT"** was rejected: the
assets here are physical equipment, not IT services. MTBF/MTTR and the
equipment register are textbook asset-management instrumentation.

## How to Drive It

Use the **run-odoo** skill (`odoo shell`); there are no routes to `curl`.

```python
# Corrective vs preventive requests on the kanban pipeline
env['maintenance.request'].search([], limit=5).mapped(
    ('name', 'maintenance_type', 'stage_id.name'))

# Equipment reliability KPIs (computed in maintenance.mixin)
env['maintenance.equipment'].search([], limit=5).mapped(
    ('name', 'mtbf', 'mttr', 'estimated_next_failure'))

# Prove preventive recurrence (write(), not a cron): completing a recurring
# preventive request copies the next occurrence
r = env['maintenance.request'].create({
    'name': 'PM', 'maintenance_type': 'preventive', 'recurring_maintenance': True,
    'repeat_interval': 1, 'repeat_unit': 'month',
    'schedule_date': '2026-06-20 09:00:00'})
r.stage_id = env.ref('maintenance.stage_3')              # a done=True stage
env['maintenance.request'].search_count([('name', '=', 'PM')])   # expect 2
```
